# Linear

- **URL:** https://linear.app
- **Screenshots viewed:** `shots/linear-hero.png`, `shots/linear.png`

## One plain sentence
A single tool where software teams (and now AI agents) plan, track, and ship product work.

## The hero
- **Headline:** "The product development system for teams and agents"
- **Subhead:** "Purpose-built for planning and building products. Designed for the AI era." (with a "New - Loops ->" pointer to the right)
- **CTA:** No big button in the hero band itself; "Sign up" sits top-right in the nav. Below the headline sits a large, crisp product screenshot (an issue "DRV-8852 Faster app launch" with an agent panel running "Opus 5").
- **Why it lands (partly):** Short, declarative, confident. "Product development system" is clear to anyone in tech; the product shot shows exactly what you get. For a true layman outside software, "product development system" and the issue-tracker UI are opaque - this hero assumes a builder audience and does not over-explain.

## Landing structure (from full-page shot)
1. Hero headline + subhead over a hero product screenshot.
2. Customer logo row (OpenAI, Vercel, Figma, Cursor, Coinbase, Ramp...).
3. "A new species of product tool" - positioning statement + three line-art concept tiles (purpose-built / powered by agents / designed for speed).
4. "Intake and integrations" - two-column: heading + explainer left, product UI right.
5. "Planning and monitoring" - same pattern.
6. "AI and automations" - same pattern.
7. "Build, review, and ship" - code-diff UI.
8. "Changelog" grid.
9. Footer.

## Branding
- **Color:** Near-black background throughout, white/gray text, restrained. Occasional accent (a single yellow star, subtle purple). Extremely monochrome.
- **Typography:** Clean grotesque sans, large tight headline, generous spacing. Feels engineered and precise.
- **Logo/mark:** Circular "swirl" glyph + "Linear" wordmark.
- **Tone:** Minimal, premium, insider. Lets the product UI do the talking.

## UX / patterns worth borrowing
- The repeated two-column rhythm: short verbal claim on one side, an actual product screenshot on the other. Every claim is shown, not just asserted.
- Dark canvas makes bright product UI screenshots pop - strong figure/ground.
- Consistent section grammar (title + one sentence + visual) makes a long page feel calm.
- A logo row directly under the hero for instant credibility.

## What NOT to borrow
- Near-zero plain-language explanation - Linear can assume its audience already knows the category; babit cannot.
- No hero CTA button (only nav "Sign up") - fine for a famous brand, risky for a product a layman is meeting for the first time.
- All-dark, all-monochrome can read as cold/abstract if you have no concrete story yet.
