# Stripe

- **URL:** https://stripe.com
- **Screenshots viewed:** `shots/stripe-hero.png` (this captured a scrolled/expanded "Payments" sub-panel, not the top hero), `shots/stripe.png` (full-page; the true top hero is visible here)

## One plain sentence
The software plumbing that lets any business accept payments and run money operations online.

## The hero (from the top of the full-page shot)
- **Headline:** "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue" (with "grow your revenue" in Stripe's signature multicolor gradient).
- **Subhead:** "Accept payments, offer financial services, and implement custom revenue models - from your first transaction to your billionth."
- **CTAs:** primary button + "See our pricing" text link; a logo row (Amazon, and others).
- **Why it lands (mixed):** "from your first transaction to your billionth" is a great plain-English range that signals "works whether you're tiny or huge." But "financial infrastructure" and "custom revenue models" are abstract - a layman knows "accept payments" but not "infrastructure." Stripe deliberately targets builders, so it accepts some abstraction.

## The `stripe-hero.png` sub-panel (what actually rendered)
An expanded Payments product card:
- **Sub-headline:** "Accept and optimize payments globally - online and in person"
- **Sub-subhead:** "Simplify your checkout, expand your reach, and boost conversion with AI-powered tools to accept payments anywhere."
- Right-side checklist (seamless checkout, expand to new markets, maximize acceptance/mitigate fraud, unify online and in-person).
- **Vivid product mocks:** a phone checkout screen (Apple Pay, Pay with Link, card options) and a stat card "3.8% increase in authorization rates on average with Authorization Boost," plus a Fraud metrics card. Realistic, detailed, trustworthy UI.

## Landing structure (from full-page shot)
1. Hero (financial infrastructure).
2. Customer logo row (Amazon...).
3. "Flexible solutions for every business model" with the expandable Payments panel (the part captured).
4. "Building the economic infrastructure for AI" band (dark, gradient).
5. Further modular solution cards lower down (several did not fully render in the capture).

## Branding
- **Color:** Light, airy off-white/lavender base; the famous fluid multicolor gradient (indigo -> orange -> pink) as the signature brand device on key words and section backgrounds. Otherwise very restrained.
- **Typography:** Sohne-style clean grotesque; large, tight, confident headlines; excellent hierarchy.
- **Logo/mark:** "stripe" wordmark.
- **Tone:** Polished, authoritative, "the infrastructure everyone builds on." Design is a category benchmark.

## UX / patterns worth borrowing
- **Realistic, high-fidelity product mockups** (a real-looking checkout, real metrics) that make an abstract backend tangible and credible.
- Concrete outcome stat inside the visual ("3.8% increase in authorization rates") rather than as a bare claim.
- Range framing ("first transaction to your billionth") to say "fits any size."
- Signature gradient used sparingly on a few words - one memorable brand device, lots of white space.
- Expandable solution panels let a broad platform stay tidy.

## What NOT to borrow
- "Financial infrastructure" / "custom revenue models" - Stripe can be abstract because "payments" is universally understood; babit's category is not, so babit must be more literal.
- The multi-product expandable complexity suits a mature platform, not a focused new product.
